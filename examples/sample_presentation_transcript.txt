[00:00:03] Dr. Aris Thorne: Hello everyone, and welcome to today's architectural keynote on HyperIndex v4, our next-generation distributed vector search engine. Over the next twenty minutes, I am going to walk you through the five slides in this deck and show a live benchmark readout demonstrating how we scaled billion-vector similarity search while cutting memory consumption in half.

[00:00:35] Dr. Aris Thorne: Starting here on Slide 1, titled "The Billion-Vector Memory Wall." Over the past eighteen months, our enterprise search cluster grew from 120 million embeddings to 1.4 billion 1536-dimensional floating-point vectors. As you can see on the left chart of Slide 1, storing uncompressed FP32 HNSW graphs in DRAM required 11.2 terabytes of cluster memory across 180 high-memory nodes. At peak query loads of 18,000 queries per second, garbage collection pauses and cross-node scatter-gather fan-out pushed our p99 query latency to 142 milliseconds, well above our 30-millisecond search tier target.

[00:01:38] Dr. Aris Thorne: Moving to Slide 2, titled "Two-Tier Hybrid Storage and RabitQ Quantization." To break the memory wall without sacrificing recall, we redesigned the storage engine around a two-tier hybrid layout. In Tier 1, which sits entirely in CPU L3 cache and DRAM, we compress the 1536-dimensional vectors using asymmetric binary quantization with corrective scalar residuals, reducing the in-memory footprint by 64 percent down to 4.0 terabytes across just 64 nodes. In Tier 2, shown at the bottom of the diagram on Slide 2, the full-precision FP16 vectors reside on local NVMe solid-state drives arranged in 16-kilobyte aligned blocks. During a query, the engine traverses the compressed in-memory graph to identify the top 250 candidate IDs, and then issues a single batched io_uring NVMe read to rescore only those 250 candidates in full precision.

[00:02:55] Dr. Aris Thorne: Now let's look at Slide 3, "Zero-Copy Kernel Bypass and SIMD Distance Kernels." When we profiled the NVMe rescoring step, we discovered that standard Linux page cache copies consumed 38 percent of CPU cycles. As illustrated in the pipeline diagram on Slide 3, we bypassed the kernel page cache entirely using direct asynchronous io_uring submission queues paired with pre-pinned hugepage ring buffers. Once the 250 full-precision vectors arrive in the ring buffer, our custom AVX-512 SIMD inner-product kernel computes exact cosine distances at a sustained throughput of 1.8 billion vector comparisons per second per core.

[00:04:02] Dr. Aris Thorne: Turning to Slide 4, "Dynamic Predicate Pushdown During Graph Traversal." A common failure mode in vector databases is post-filtering: if a user filters by a tenant ID or date range that matches only 1 percent of documents, running a top-K vector search first and filtering afterward returns nearly empty results. On Slide 4, you can see our adaptive bitmap pushdown engine. Before graph traversal begins, the query planner evaluates the cardinality of the metadata filter using Roaring Bitmaps. If the filter selectivity is above 15 percent, the engine evaluates the bitmap inline during HNSW graph hops. If selectivity is below 2 percent, it automatically switches to a bitmap-driven brute-force scan over the quantized vectors, guaranteeing 100 percent filter completeness with zero recall degradation.

[00:05:18] Dr. Aris Thorne: Finally, on Slide 5, "Production Telemetry and Live Benchmark Readout." On the screen right now is the live Grafana dashboard from our `us-east` canary cluster running a 1.4-billion vector dataset under 20,000 concurrent queries per second. Looking at the top-left panel, Recall@10 measures 98.4 percent compared to exact k-NN ground truth. Looking at the latency histogram in the center panel, median p50 latency is 4.8 milliseconds and p99 tail latency is 16.2 milliseconds—an 88 percent latency reduction compared to our legacy v3 cluster. Furthermore, because we dropped from 180 nodes to 64 nodes, total annual compute and memory expenditure decreased by $1.14 million per year. That concludes the presentation walkthrough.
